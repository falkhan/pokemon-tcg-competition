## 2026-07-20 ~12:15 — the gust "defect" was mostly MY measurement artifact

`m21_gust_value.py`, 142 real gust opportunities (60 games, B3 net):
- **Bench targets are 1-prize in 138/142 cases** (2-prize: 0, 3-prize: 4).
- **In 78/142 the opponent's ACTIVE was ALSO KO-able that same turn**; of
  those, KOing the active was worth MORE prizes 38×, equal 38×, and gust
  worth more only **2×**. I.e. in over half the "missed gust" states the
  agent had a strictly-better-or-equal line and took it.
- Only **64/142 (45%)** are states where the active can't be KO'd and gust
  is a genuine tempo gain — and even there Boss burns the turn's supporter.
- **Conclusion: the 0/27 and 0/217 "gust defect" over-counted badly.** My
  opportunity definition (uncharged benched mon I can KO) never priced the
  opportunity cost. The true miss rate is at most ~45% of what I reported,
  and the per-instance value is usually 1 prize vs 2-3 for the alternative.
  The agent declining these is often CORRECT play, not a blind spot.
  Retreat (9/139, genuinely improved) was the real defect of the pair.
- Honest revision of the M21 premise: Piotr's replay observation was a
  valid single-game read (that specific board had a 3-prize Mega ex on the
  bench — the 4/142 tail), but I generalized it into a systematic defect
  without pricing the alternative line. Lesson: **a behavioral "defect"
  metric must model the opportunity cost of the action it demands**, else
  it manufactures a phantom defect and an entire milestone leg chases it.
