## 2026-07-22 ~22:45 — Determinism check: 🔴 MY ALARM WAS WRONG. The statistics were sound all along.

Piotr asked me to fix the measuring issue. Investigated it properly and it is
**not a bug, and not a source of hidden noise** — I mis-diagnosed it.

**Root cause of the 0.660-vs-0.710 pair.** `cg.game.battle_start(deck0, deck1)`
takes **no seed**, and `cg` / `cg.game` / `cg.api` expose no seeding hook at all
(checked by introspection). The matchrunner's `--seed` only namespaces pilot
`instance` names and the checkpoint run-key; it has never controlled game
randomness. `rl/matchrunner.py:728` already says so in as many words: *"the
engine's own RNG drives game variance either way — repeated runs are independent
samples."* So two "seeds" were never paired replicates — they are 2×200
independent games, which makes pooling them **correct** and the binomial MDE
**correct**.

**Verified it is genuinely binomial** (`scripts/m28_determinism_check.sh`) —
the same spec run 6× at n=200:

| | value |
|---|---|
| runs | 0.665 0.680 0.630 0.642 0.645 0.625 |
| mean / spread | 0.6479 / 0.055 |
| observed sd | **0.0210** |
| binomial sd `sqrt(p(1-p)/n)` | **0.0338** |
| ratio | **0.62×** |
| dispersion test | χ²=1.93, df=5, **p=0.858 → consistent with binomial** |

Observed variance is *at or below* binomial. **There is no overdispersion.**
Every MDE quoted in M27 and M28 is right, every "not resolved" verdict stands,
and the two resolved kills (MCTS z=−3.17, PPO z=−2.63) are if anything
conservative.

**The correction I owe:** I wrote above that "our pooled 2-seed numbers carry
more noise than the binomial MDE implies". That was wrong — I saw two
independent samples 5pp apart (a 1.5σ event, entirely ordinary) and inferred a
defect. The 0.615-twice coincidence I cited as evidence of partial determinism
was just a coincidence. Recorded because a false alarm about instrument validity
is exactly the kind of thing that gets cargo-culted into later milestones.

**The real, standing process fix** is terminology, not code: stop calling them
"seeds" as if they were reproducible replicates. They are independent samples;
the only lever on precision is **more games**. Renaming aside, nothing in the
measurement stack needs changing.

| arm | data | baseline sign-acc | trained | delta | n_val | gate ≥0.75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `onpolicy` | 20,911 states / 300 games | 0.660 | **0.714** | **+5.4pp** | 3,065 | ❌ fail |

Both arms miss the pre-registered **≥0.75** bar. Recorded as a **kill** on
Track E as specified — no goalpost moving.

**Three things this actually establishes:**

1. **The auxiliary head was never "≈ chance".** Its honest baseline here is
   **0.660 / 0.643**, not the 0.62 the docstring quotes for the v1 era. The
   "our critic is at chance" premise I used to justify Track E was overstated —
   the head is mediocre, not broken.
2. **The docstring's ~0.87 is not reproducible under an honest split.**
   Supervised training on the same frozen features buys **0.660 → 0.714** when
   train/val is split **by game**. The historical 0.87 was measured with the
   per-state split (fixed in E1b) that leaks the outcome label across the
   split. So the headline number that made this track look attractive was a
   measurement artifact, and the real ceiling of this technique on this lineage
   is ~0.71.
3. **🔴 The "documented-but-never-executed fix" fails, and the reason is
   conceptual, not a bug.** Training on determinized search states does not
   merely under-deliver — it drives the head from 0.643 down to **0.527**,
   barely above chance. Why: `_search_visited` labels every *hypothetical*
   state reached by a determinized rollout with the **actual game's** outcome.
   That label is only valid if the determinization is faithful, and ours fills
   the opponent's hidden zones with Snorlax/energy placeholders and takes
   random actions half the time. So the search-state corpus is 90k rows of
   near-duplicate states carrying **labels from a game that never happened** —
   noise with a confident sign. It swamps the 20k honest rows by 4:1.

   The M8.4-era note ("the value head was OOD on determinized search states —
   so train it on exactly those states") diagnosed the symptom correctly and
   prescribed the wrong cure: you cannot fix OOD-ness by fitting to
   mislabelled data. A correct version needs outcome labels obtained by
   **rolling the determinized line out to termination**, not by borrowing the
   real game's result.

**Consequence for the campaign.** The "bad leaf" explanation for M12 / M22 /
M28-B is *weakened*, not confirmed: the leaf is 0.66–0.71, and the best honest
improvement available to us is ~5pp. That is very unlikely to flip MCTS's
−11.0pp. Lookahead's failure is looking less like "the leaf is broken" and more
like "a 0.7-accuracy leaf is simply not good enough to beat trusting a
0.67-fidelity policy", which is a much harder problem than retraining a head.

| arm | leaf sign-acc | seeds | pooled n=400 | vs no-search | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no search (rung 0) | 0.660 | 0.660 0.615 | **0.6375** | — | — |

**Improving the leaf by +5.4pp of sign-accuracy made search 14.8pp WORSE.**
Both seeds agree; z=−7.54 is the largest effect measured in the campaign.

**Why this is coherent rather than paradoxical, and why it closes the axis.**
The supervised head is trained by MSE on *on-policy* states, where it reaches
0.714. MCTS does not evaluate on-policy states — it evaluates determinized,
several-ply-deep hypotheticals. On those, the retrained head is **confidently
wrong**, while the old auxiliary head was closer to uninformative. PUCT weights
a confident-but-wrong leaf much more heavily than a flat one, so sharpening the
leaf on the wrong distribution amplifies the error instead of correcting it.

That is the same OOD gap E3's `search` arm tried to close — and could not,
because labelling hypothetical states with the *real* game's outcome is invalid
(0.643 → 0.527). So both directions are now measured and both fail:

- train the leaf on-policy → better on-policy, **much worse in search**
- train the leaf on search states → **worse everywhere** (labels are noise)

**LOOKAHEAD IS CLOSED, and the leaf excuse is spent.** M12 (solver), M22
(`solved:`), M28-B (MCTS, old leaf), M28-E4 (MCTS, better leaf) — four
measurements, monotonically worse as we invest more in the leaf. `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s
"the bottleneck is `score_leaf` itself" survives, but with a sharper edge: the
bottleneck is not the leaf's *accuracy*, it is that **no leaf trained on
reachable data is valid on the states search actually visits.** Reopening
requires labels generated by rolling determinized lines to termination — i.e.
real self-play inside the search distribution — which is an AlphaZero-scale
loop, not a head retrain.

| `m28_control` (full corpus) | 0.6925 (+3.0pp) | +0.91 | 0.3525 (+1.0pp) | +0.30 |
| `m28_phase` (full + phase ×4) | 0.6813 (+1.9pp) | +0.56 | 0.3625 (+2.0pp) | +0.59 |
| **`m28_winners`** | **0.7256 n=800 (+6.3pp)** | **+2.22 ✅ RESOLVED** | 0.3300 n=800 (−1.3pp) | −0.43 |
| `m28_wphase` (winners + phase ×4) | 0.6525 (−1.0pp) | −0.30 | 0.3075 (−3.5pp) | −1.06 |

**F — winners-only WORKS, and it replicated.** First look was 0.7288 at n=400
(z=+2.04, right on the boundary). Given this session already produced one
textbook regression to the mean, I added seeds 3–4 before claiming it: it held
at **0.7256 over n=800, z=+2.22**, with all four seeds (0.718 / 0.740 / 0.705 /
0.740) above the pin. The out-of-loop first look of −3.3pp *also* regressed, to
−1.3pp (z=−0.43) — flat.

So: **dropping the 45% of demonstrations where the teacher lost buys a resolved
+6.3pp on the mirror at no measurable out-of-loop cost**, from a corpus with
*half* the data. It is a one-flag change that has been available since M23.

**Three honest caveats, because this is the only positive result we have:**

1. `rule:lucario` is a **training-distribution regression check, not a
   generalization floor** (measure-agent skill; `docs/DECISIONS.md` 2026-07-20).
   A mirror gain says "we learned our training distribution better" — which is
   exactly what cleaner demonstrations *should* buy — and is the weaker kind of
   evidence.
2. The only doubly-clean instrument, `rule:dragapult`, is **flat**. There is no
   evidence the gain generalizes.
3. No offline gate has demonstrated live-predictive validity (M22 audit). This
   is a real offline result, not a predicted live one.

**G — phase conditioning is DEAD on both halves.** `m28_phase` is inside noise
of the control on both axes (+1.9pp / +2.0pp, z<0.6), and the phase weight
actively *destroys* the winners gain: 0.7256 → 0.6525, i.e. it gives back all
6.3pp and more. Probe 2's phase-dependent val_acc is real, but it is **not a
fixable representational gap** — the late-game states are simply harder, and
spending gradient on them costs more elsewhere than it buys. The M27
"features × weighting" pattern did **not** replicate here; that pattern is not
a general law, it worked for supporters because the supporter defect was a
base-rate problem, and phase-competence is not.
