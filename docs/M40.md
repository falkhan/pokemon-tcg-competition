## 2026-08-02 — X0 accrual audit: reads are PARALLEL, but n=150 has never been reachable

`scripts/m40_accrual.py` (NEW) over all 28 cached listings. Two questions; the
plan only asked the first, and the second is the one that binds.

### 1. Does accrual split across live submissions? NO — the §4 kill does not fire

The naive test (group days by `k` = submissions live that day) is **confounded
by age** and gives contradictory answers depending on the day subset — all-days
says per-sub throughput *rises* from k=1 (25.5) to k=4 (33.7), interior-days
says it *falls* from 26.0 to 16.2. New submissions are launched exactly as old
ones decay, so `k` is a proxy for "how many young subs are live", not for any
sharing rule. Both tables are printed and neither is the answer.

The age-controlled test is clean: a submission's **day-0 burst**, conditioned on
how many others were already live. Age is fixed at 0 by construction.

| others live | subs | mean day-0 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 26.0 |
| 1 | 2 | 30.5 |
| 2 | 9 | **43.0** |
| 3 | 6 | **47.5** |
| 4 | 10 | 24.7 |

Burst ratio 0-others → 4-others is **0.95**, against 0.20 under a hard team
split. Nine submissions launched with two others already live averaged 43 games
on day 0; on 2026-07-23 three of our submissions went live the same day and took
52 / 56 / 48. **Accrual is per-submission. Parallel reads are real**, and the
pre-registered kill ("accrual splits hard → revert to sequential ships") does
not fire.

### 2. The constraint the plan never priced: submissions DIE, and n=150 is unreachable

Accrual is not a rate, it is a decay curve. Mean games by submission age, over
the 25 submissions whose life has finished:

| age (days) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean games | **36.4** | 21.3 | 16.6 | 9.4 | 13.5 | 8.0 |

Median lifetime **2 days**. Lifetime totals: **min 36, median 50, max 112.**

**No submission in campaign history has reached n=150.** Verified against the
LIVE Kaggle API, not the cache — refetching four dead submissions returns
exactly their cached rows:

| sub | live rows now | newest episode | shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 54621283 | 112 | 2026-07-17 | 41/21/16/10/12/12 then zero for 16 days |
| 54914673 (M29 ship) | 52 | 2026-07-23 | all 52 in ONE day, then dead |
| 55065484 (M37) | 94 | 2026-07-31 | 35/32/25/2 then dead |
| 55146658 (M38) | 76 | 2026-08-02 | 44/26/6, decaying now |

So the decay is real and is not an artifact of the monitor notebook's 3-day
refresh window.

### What this changes

**G-10's declared read-n of 150 is not a dwell, it is an unreachable bar.** M39
declared n=150 for BOTH Ship A and Ship B before sampling (correctly, by the
rule as written) — those reads can never resolve. Ship A is at 57 and Ship B at
35 today, and on the observed curve they will top out near 100 and 90.

What is actually attainable per submission, and what it buys:

| n | 95% CI at p=0.5 | implied ELO (ELO_PER_WR=700) |
|---|---|---|
| 50 (median) | ±13.9pp | **±97** |
| 110 (best ever) | ±9.3pp | **±65** |

**The two findings combine into the M40 read discipline.** Because accrual is
per-submission and slots are 5/day, the way to reach decision-grade n is not to
wait longer — waiting gets nothing, the submission is dead — it is to **submit
the same config more than once and pool the reads**. That reframes §S4.3's
"replication ship" completely: it was scoped as a measurement of ladder read
noise, and it is in fact the *only mechanism by which a live claim can reach
n≥150 at all*. Two replicates of one config buy a tighter interval than one
config watched twice as long, because the second watch yields zero games.

Flagged to Piotr rather than acted on: G-10 needs restating (declare a read-n
that is attainable, or declare a replicate count), and the M40 plan's §3 budget
table — which prices reads at 10/30/55 games/day against a 42-day calendar —
is measuring the wrong axis.

2. **The wall bed never serves a non-zero plan.** In the failed first run, all

   argmaxes to the NULL plan (`encode_plan(None)` is all-zeros) essentially
   always. So the beds are already, accidentally, serving what they were
   trained on — and our pilot is the one out of distribution.

**Every pre-existing check passes on the live shipped agent and 6a fails.**
That is the M24-to-M40 regression rendered visible for the first time, and it
is the exact demonstration that the existing gate could not have caught it.

Four sub-checks, each aimed at a defect class rather than at today's accident:

- **6a plan parity** — thresholds are not symmetric, because a plan-CARRYING
  corpus does not measure 1.0: `encode_plan(None)` is all-zeros and the null
  plan is a frequent legitimate choice. Measured on the three plan-carrying
  corpora we own (`plan_m16` / `plan_m18` / `plan_m19_rw2x`): **0.159 / 0.180
  / 0.193**, against exactly **0.000** for every replay-derived corpus. Three
  orders of magnitude apart, so ≥0.05-when-serving / ≤0.01-when-zeroed is not
  a close call. Both directions fail: zeroing against a plan-carrying corpus
  is also a mismatch.
- **6b encoder width, per dir, never pooled** — pooling is precisely what
  `BCDatasetV3` does at train time, and it is what hides the mismatch.
- **6c option width** — the bundle truncates wide options, so wider is fine;
  narrower means the net's tail option columns saw nothing in training.
- **6d v4 integrity** — `OppMemory.features` sets `v[3 + hot] = 1.0`
  unconditionally, so offset 3 of the v4 block is 1.0 in *every* vector
  `encode_ctx_v4` can emit and 0.0 in a zero-padded v3 row. One float
  comparison detects a v3 row masquerading as v4 — the §S5 hazard above.

`--corpus` is REQUIRED, not optional: a skippable parity check is not a
guardrail, and both regressions survived because nothing forced anyone to look.

Coverage added where the gap actually was: `tests/test_plan_serve.py` (7 tests)
is the **first coverage anywhere of fn3/fn4's plan state machine** — the absence
of which is why this lived sixteen milestones — plus `tests/test_ship_verify.py`
(17 tests) pinning check 6's decision boundary on synthesized bundles rather

One test-design note worth keeping: the first version of the "as-shipped feeds

## 2026-08-02 — S5: the walker lands and the FIDELITY PROBE PASSES at 100.0000%

The lane's gate. `docs/M40-plan.md` §4 pre-registers "walker-corrected fidelity
probe fails → kill the re-encode", so this decides whether S5 proceeds at all.

**Built:**

- `rl/replay_bc.py:iter_replay_prompts` — the full-prompt walker. Keeps only the
  `status != ACTIVE` filter, yields every own prompt tagged with its drop
  reason. `iter_replay_decisions` is now a thin filter over it, so the drop
  taxonomy, its evaluation order and the counters are bit-identical and all 13
  call sites are untouched (pinned by
  `tests/test_replay_walker.py::test_decisions_is_exactly_the_unfiltered_walker`).
- `rl/replay_bc.py:encode_decisions_v4` — observes `OppMemory` on EVERY own
  prompt, resets (and does **not** observe) on the deck prompt, emits rows only
  at label-bearing prompts so row counts stay one-for-one with the v3 corpus it
  replaces. `build --v4` writes 1702-wide states / 25 ids, and
  `deck_registry.json` now records `enc_ver` — the only durable record of a
  shard dir's encoder version, which G-14 6b needs because `BCDatasetV3`'s pad
  shim makes the inferred version ambiguous.

**The probe** (`scripts/m40_v4_fidelity.py`): a recorder agent logs the
observation dict it was handed, in call order, to a side channel; the same
games' `env.toJSON()` is replayed through the walker; the two are compared on
`OppMemory`'s **full internal state** after every prompt. Not `features()` —
that saturates at `min(x,10)/10`, so a real divergence in `_attacks_total`
11→12 would be invisible.

```
FIDELITY (gating): offline reconstruction vs the live stream
  prompts compared : 3902     exact matches : 3902     agreement : 100.0000%
PASS — the re-encode may proceed.
```

22 games × 2 seats, clearing the pre-registered bar (≥20 games, ≥2,500
prompts). The threshold was pre-registered at **100.0%, not 99%**: this is a
deterministic accumulator replayed over a recorded stream, so a divergence is
a defect and never noise, and a 99% bar would admit exactly the class of silent
drop the lane exists to remove.

**The instrument failed first, again, and this is now a pattern worth naming.**
The probe's first run reported 26.4% agreement and `COUNT off=102 live=390` —
a catastrophic-looking lane failure. The cause was that both agents in a game
append to one trace file and the run spanned four games, so four interleaved
streams were compared against one episode. Fixed by running one game per
`play_games` call with its own trace. That is the *second* time today a probe's
first result was an artifact of the probe (the S6 mechanism probe counted the
opponent's plan head). **Both would have been reported as findings by a less
suspicious reader**, and one of them would have killed a lane. The M39 lesson —
"four of my own conclusions were corrected mid-milestone, all the same shape" —
generalises: a *decisive-looking negative from a brand-new instrument* is
evidence about the instrument until the instrument is shown to be sound on a
positive control.

**Non-gating, and it is the walker's actual ROI** — how much the naive
labelled-prompts-only path corrupts, over 1,200 cached episodes:

| | |
|---|---|
| labelled rows | 172,076 |
| rows carrying a WRONG memory vector | **1,755 (1.02%)** |
| episodes affected | 28 / 1,200 (2.33%) |

Contamination (1.02% of rows) is an order of magnitude larger than the drop
rate (~0.28% of prompts) because a dropped window poisons the rest of that
seat's game. It is also **concentrated**: 2.33% of episodes carry all of it.
Two consequences — the walker is justified on its own, and the corruption a
naive v4 build would carry is *biased* rather than random, so cross-tabulating
those 28 episodes against submission id / score / game length is worth an hour
in M41 before anyone treats the residue as noise.

**What the probe cannot prove**, recorded so it is not quietly assumed: that
HARVESTED Kaggle episodes carry the same log semantics as locally generated
ones. It validates our replay path on engine-generated games and assumes
transfer. Supporting evidence for the assumption (not proof): the kaggle
interpreter writes `o["logs"] = obs["logs"]` only for the ACTIVE seat, which is
exactly the boundary the walker keeps.

## 2026-08-02 — S5 v4 arm trained: the encoder buys NOTHING in-sample

`scripts/m40_v4_train.sh` → `checkpoints/m40_v4_cont3.pt`. The arm is a
single-variable replay of `cont3`, the campaign's best-evidenced fine-tune:
same init lineage (`migrate_v3_to_v4(m28_winners)`), same seats, same 3
epochs / lr 1e-4 / SUPPORTER:5 STADIUM:5, best-val selects. Only the encoding
differs.

**A confound surfaced on the first comparison and was fixed rather than
reported.** Against the live `cont3` log the v4 arm looked ahead at init
(0.653 vs 0.631) — but both inits are the *same network* (the M21 invariant:
migration zero-inits the new columns), so a 22-point gap was impossible from
the encoder. It was the corpus: the v4 rebuild saw 245 seats / 17,035 rows
against cont3's 231 / 16,082, because M39's r3 harvest grew the cache after
cont3 was trained. The corpus was a second variable.

Fixed by rebuilding a **matched v3 control** from today's cache
(`data/bc_m38_w9294_v3new`, 245 episodes / 17,035 rows — byte-identical seat
set to the v4 corpus, differing only in encoding width) and training
`m40_v3_ctl` with the identical recipe:

| | v3 control (matched) | v4 arm |
|---|---|---|
| train / val | 15,474 / 1,561 | 15,474 / 1,561 |
| **init val_acc** | **0.653** | **0.653** |
| best val_acc | **0.666** (ep2) | 0.664 (ep0) |

The identical init val_acc is a free empirical confirmation of the M21
warm-start invariant — the migrated net *is* the original at init, measured
rather than assumed.

**The result: v4 does not beat v3 on held-out imitation accuracy.** 0.664 vs
0.666, i.e. nothing, with the v3 control marginally ahead. The 341 extra
features and 5 memory ids add no measurable in-sample signal.

This does **not** settle the lane, and the reason is on the record: M39 found
corpora with *higher* init val_acc performing *worse* in play, so val_acc is
not play strength. The mechanism claim for v4 was never "predicts the
demonstrator better" — it was "sees the opponent's bench threat and charging
target", which is a small number of decisions that uniform cross-entropy over
all prompts will drown. The pre-registered decider is the weighted panel, and
the gate is queued (`v4cont3_pkg` vs `v3ctl_pkg`, prefix `m40_pan`). But an
honest prior after this: the lane needs the gate to show something the
training curve gives no hint of.

## 2026-08-02 — S2 collector: resumable by chunk, and the exploration redesign clears the kill

`scripts/m40_s2_collect.py`. Built for interruption on Piotr's instruction,
which is the right call for the one multi-hour job in the milestone —
`plan_iter collect` has NO resume at all (relaunching clobbers the out dir),
and the standing workaround has been "collect the remainder into a FRESH dir
and train on both".

**The resume contract.** Work splits into `(bed, seed, game-range)` chunks
(default 50 games). Each chunk writes its shard, and only then commits its
manifest entry via an atomic `os.replace`. A restart replays the manifest and
runs exactly what is missing. Consequences: a crash loses at most one chunk;
pausing is just Ctrl-C; and the same command is always safe to re-run.

Verified rather than asserted, because an untested resume invites you to trust
a restart that silently drops work:

| behaviour | result |
|---|---|
| re-run when complete | "nothing to do — collection complete" |
| `--status` | progress without collecting |
| resume after a config change | **REFUSED**, with both fingerprints printed |
| crash recovery (chunk deleted from manifest) | re-ran only that chunk |
| after recovery: duplication? | none — the shard tag is deterministic, so the orphan is overwritten |
| after recovery: `game_id` collisions across chunks? | **zero** |
| corpus loads in `BCDatasetV3`? | yes — 642 rows, both win and loss rows present |

The config-fingerprint refusal matters more than it looks. Resuming a dir under
a different `--tau` would blend two corpora with different exploration
distributions into one directory, and nothing downstream could tell — the same
class of silent contamination as S5's zero-padded rows and S6's plan mismatch.

**The exploration redesign, and it clears the pre-registered bar.** M39's
`bestresp` explored by replacing the action with a UNIFORM draw over all legal
options at ε=0.08 on single-pick MAIN prompts, and produced a corpus the net
agreed with **93.5%** of the time. Two reasons: only ~5% of rows were explored
at all, and those that were mostly contained actions the net would never
consider. This collector samples from the POLICY instead — `--tau` (softmax
temperature) or `--eps` over the **top-k** — so a deviation is a plausible
alternative line.

First measurement, τ=0.6 on the grim bed:

```
agreement 0.8433    explored 0.1567
```

**0.843 against M39's 0.935**, and comfortably under the pre-registered 0.90
kill. Roughly triple the exploration rate, from actions the policy actually
ranks. The kill is wired in as `--kill-above 0.90` checked per chunk once
`--kill-after` prompts are in, and it aborts *at collection* — where the plan
says it belongs, because `runs/m39_retain_b.log` shows init val_acc 0.907 on
the milestone's best net, so the same gate at the training entry would have
killed it.

**Two design choices worth recording.** The arm's own rules stay in the loop:
the base action is whatever the full pilot (net + fix stack) plays and
exploration is a deviation FROM that, so the corpus is on-distribution for the
agent we ship rather than for a bare net. And `explored` counts actions that
*differ* from the arm's pick, not sampler invocations — otherwise a draw that

## 2026-08-02 — S3.3: per-spec solver budget, so composites can differ inside one battery

`solved:<ckpt>:<deck>[:<max_nodes>[:<deadline_ms>]]`. The budget has been a
per-CALL dict since M11 — `wrap_with_solver` → `solve_turn` → `solve_turn_line`
→ `_dfs` all take it and resolve `None` to the module constant — and only
`parse_spec` and the `solved` branch pinned it to the globals, which forced
every composite in a run to share one budget. Six lines.

Verified by instrumentation rather than by reading, because a config knob that
silently fails to act is the failure mode that has bitten three times today:

```
solved:...:grim_live            -> {'deadline_s': 0.05, 'max_nodes': 120}   (defaults)
solved:...:grim_live:800        -> {'deadline_s': 0.05, 'max_nodes': 800}
solved:...:grim_live:800:400    -> {'deadline_s': 0.4,  'max_nodes': 800}
```

Guarded the hazard the recon turned up: `make_solver_pilot` (the
`solver:`/`solver-dev:` kinds) calls `solve_turn` with **no** budget arguments,
so a budget appended to those specs would be silently ignored — a composite
that looks tightened and is not. `parse_spec` now rejects it rather than
accepting and dropping it, with a test.

**The real corpus immediately falsified the G-14 6d check.** As first written
it asserted `states[:, 1361+3] == 1.0` on the grounds that
`OppMemory.features` sets that offset unconditionally. It does not: the write
is `v[3 + hot] = 1.0` with `hot ∈ 0..6`, and offset 3 is only the *"no attach
seen yet"* slot — it goes cold the moment the opponent attaches an energy. On
the first real v4 shard **3,629 of 5,075 rows "failed" a corpus that is
perfectly clean.** The correct invariant is that the one-hot at offsets 3..9
SUMS to 1.0, which is 0.0 for a zero-padded row exactly as intended. Fixed;
the real corpus now reads 0 failures over 17,035 rows.

Worth recording *why* the unit tests did not catch this: the fixture built its
synthetic v4 rows by setting offset 3, i.e. it encoded the same misreading as
the check, so the test agreed with the bug. The fixture now varies which slot
is hot per row. **A test written from the same mental model as the code under
test verifies the model, not the code** — the third instrument-before-result
correction today, after the S6 mechanism probe and the S5 fidelity probe.
