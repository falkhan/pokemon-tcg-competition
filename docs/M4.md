# M4 — Deck Optimization (the meta): self-play deck search

**Status:** 🚧 Active (started 2026-07-08)
**Plan/why:** After M1–M3, no *pilot* we train beats bc_v1+Kyogre, and the leaderboard shows
we're at 640 vs ~1100+ at the top. The lever we never pulled is the **deck**. Deck quality is
largely orthogonal to pilot skill — a strong deck piloted decently beats a weak deck piloted
perfectly — and optimizing a 60-card deck over a huge combinatorial space is an optimization
problem, the natural home of self-play RL / evolutionary search. This is RL-central *and* the
highest-EV path to climbing.

## The approach (hybrid, deliberately)

- **Pilot = the rule expert** (Lucario), a strong, fixed baseline. Its card-specific heuristics
  fire on the archetype core; unknown flex cards get generic play (graceful fallback, verified).
- **Optimize the deck** by self-play: mutate the deck, play variants against each other, rate
  with **openskill**, keep the best, repeat. Archetype-preserving — the Pokémon engine is fixed
  (the core), the ~44 trainer/energy "flex" slots are searched (how real TCG deckbuilding works).
- **Deck vs deck is piloted by the SAME agent both sides**, slot-fair → isolates deck strength
  from pilot noise.

## Assumptions (tracked)

| # | Assumption | Test | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deck quality materially affects win rate under a fixed strong pilot | search finds a variant beating the seed ≥55% slot-fair | ⏳ |
| 2 | The rule pilot handles flex mutations gracefully (core heuristics fire) | no crashes; sane play | ⏳ |
| 3 | An optimized deck + rule pilot beats bc_v1+Kyogre (our champion) and climbs the board | eval + submit | ⏳ |
| 4 | Deck search is cheap enough (rule agent, direct engine loop, ~50k games/hr) | generations in minutes | ⏳ |

## Components (rl/deck_search.py)

- `validate_deck`, `mutate` — exist. Add `mutate_flex(deck, core)` (fix Pokémon core).
- `matchup(deckA, deckB, n_games)` — fast direct-engine-loop, rule-agent both sides, slot-fair;
  returns per-game winners.
- `rate_population(decks, ...)` — random pairings + openskill ratings.
- `evolve(seed_deck, pop, generations)` — mutation-bandit: each gen rate, replace bottom quartile
  with mutations of the top quartile. Returns the best deck.

## Milestones

## Results log

- **M4.0 ✅** infra built; **M4.1 ✅** sanity (mirror 15/15/0 slot-fair; mutate_flex keeps 16
  Pokémon; openskill ranks the tuned seed above random mutations).
- **M4.2a ❌ (method bug):** the openskill `evolve()` selected a "best" deck that actually
  *lost to the seed 44%* — population ratings at small samples (4 opp × 8 games) are too noisy
  to rank near-identical variants, and promoted a worse deck by chance (it even cut all 4 Dusk
  Balls). **Fix:** `hill_climb()` — propose a flex mutation, play it vs the current champion over
  150 games, accept only if it clears 57% (a clear win, not noise). Cannot regress; honest.
- **M4.2b hill-climb (vs mirror):** found 1 accepted mutation; evolved deck beats the seed
  **60%** (n=200) — BUT this is **mirror-overfit**: vs Iono the evolved deck is **79%** vs the
  seed's **82%** (flat/worse). Optimizing against your own mirror tunes anti-mirror tech
  (Heavy Baton + Wally's Compassion for a Gravity Mountain + Fighting Energy), not general strength.
- **M4.2c (fix) diverse-field search ✅ honest:** seed fitness (avg win vs Iono + bc_v1 champion)
  = **0.84** already; **0/30** flex mutations improved it. The Lucario deck is well-tuned; no
  generalizing improvement exists against the field we can simulate. (Confirms the mirror 60% was
  overfitting.)
- **M4.3 not run:** the Lucario *rule agent + tuned deck* already beats everything we've built
  (82% vs Iono, ~86% vs bc_v1). We can't improve the deck (search says so), and the real 4500-team
  leaderboard field is not simulable — so deck search is bottlenecked by opponent diversity, not
  by our method.

## Verdict

Deck search is **robust and honest** — it finds real signal when it exists (mirror) and correctly
finds *nothing* when a deck is already tuned (diverse field). But it can't manufacture a better
deck than a hand-tuned one when the only opponents to optimize against are 2 rule pilots + bc_v1.
Combined with M1–M3, the comprehensive finding stands: **the provided rule agents + tuned decks
are stronger than anything we can build OR improve on this budget.** Next is a strategy call
(see DECISIONS.md), not another experiment.

**Method notes (both important):**
1. Self-play deck *ranking* needs large samples — near-identical decks differ by a few % win
   rate, swamped by noise. Direct-vs-champion hill-climbing (big N) beats noisy population openskill.
2. Deck fitness must be vs a **diverse fixed field, not the mirror** — mirror-only search overfits
   to beating copies of itself (same failure mode as PPO mirror self-play). Our diverse field is
   small (2 rule pilots + bc_v1), which bounds how well improvements generalize to the real
   4500-team leaderboard.

## Known risks

- If the rule pilot plays all archetype variants near-identically, flex differences may be small
  and noisy → needs many games per matchup to detect signal.
- Search may overfit to beating its own population (self-play meta) rather than the real field.
- "Rule pilot + optimized deck" is a hybrid, not a pure neural agent — intentional; RL lives in
  the deck-optimization loop (+ optional value/MCTS in M4.4).
