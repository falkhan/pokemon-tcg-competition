|---|---|---|

### 🔴 KILL: neither instrument has demonstrated live-predictive validity

## 2026-07-20 ~15:00 — 🔴 every gate opponent is in the training pool

Prompted by Piotr asking what "contamination" actually means. Pinning the term down surfaced a
defect nobody had noticed.

**Definition.** Contamination = train/test leakage with *opponents* as the data. If we train
against X and then gate on performance vs X, a high score no longer means "strong agent" — it can
mean "learned X's quirks." The gate stops being a test and becomes a training-progress readout.

**Audit of B3's live mixture (`runs/m21_legB3.log:78`) against our gates:**

| gate | opponent | weight in B3 training |
|---|---|---|
| mirror (canonical) | `solver:lucario` | **0.45** |
| meta co-gate | the 4 meta decks | 0.55 |
| floor | `random:kyogre` | 0.05 |
| floor | `rule:lucario` | 0.05 |

**Nothing is held out. Not one gate.**

### 🐛 The 0.45 is a double-count, and it is a real bug

The mixture lists `solver:data/kaggle/meta_v2/deck_20dcd3130bc0.csv=0.30` and
`solver:lucario=0.15` as if they were two different opponents. They are **the same opponent** —
`deck_20dcd3130bc0.csv` is byte-identical to `decks/lucario.csv` (md5 `aef8da62…`), same solver
pilot. So **45% of B3's training was against our solver piloting our exact deck**, written as two
entries, and the mirror gate measures precisely that.

The same collapse that made the meta gate identical to the mirror gate had *already happened
inside the training mixture*, unnoticed. The declared weights do not mean what they say.

**Action for M22b:** rewrite the mixture so weights are deduplicated by resolved (deck, pilot)
identity, and consciously reallocate the freed mass. `parse_pool` (`rl/collector.py:119-148`)
should reject or merge duplicate resolved specs rather than silently summing them.

### Two problems were hiding under one word

1. **Leakage** — trained on the evaluator. *Fixable by holding something out.*
2. **Endogeneity** — the evaluator is our own artifact (our solver, our past selves). Beating it
   says nothing about other teams' agents, however carefully it is held out. *Not fixable by
   holding out.*

`rule:lucario`, `random:kyogre`, `solver:iono`, `generic` and the meta decks are contaminated in
sense 1. Everything solver-piloted is endogenous in sense 2 regardless.

**`dragapult` is the only opponent we own that is clean on both** — never in `collector.py`,
`plan_iter.py` or `ppo.py`, and written by The Pokémon Company rather than by us.

**Correction to the previous recommendation:** holding out `drakloak` as a replacement evaluator
was proposed as "strictly better, since it appears in the live field." That was wrong — a
held-out drakloak is still *solver-piloted*, so it fixes leakage only and stays inside the closed
loop. It is a partial substitute, not an equivalent one. There is no way to manufacture another
doubly-clean opponent: we cannot write an agent we did not write.

### Decision (Piotr, 07-20): dragapult stays OUT of training

Reversing the earlier "add it with sequencing" plan. Dragapult is reserved as the out-of-loop
evaluator for at least one more leg, and used to test whether M22b's PPO scaling generalizes
beyond the closed loop. Training against it would spend a non-renewable instrument to buy
opponent diversity we can approximate with held-out harvested archetypes.

### ⚠️ The caveat that keeps this honest, and is baked into the tool's output

Flags are counted over **whole games**, so they are confounded with game state: a game you are
winning has more turns, more energy and a wider board, which moves flag rates for reasons that
have nothing to do with causation. **A negative delta does not mean the behaviour helps.**

So this is a *filter, not a cause-finder*. It can refute "flag X explains our losses"; it cannot
establish that X helps or hurts. `report_flags` prints that caveat unconditionally and a test
pins it (`test_report_always_carries_the_confounding_caveat`), so the table cannot be quoted
without it.

### Why — the architecture, not the training

`submission/main.py:143-193` is the whole decision path: encode → one plan argmax over ≤48
candidates → one `score_options_v3` pass → `argsort`. **Zero engine calls at inference.**

`rl/turn_solver.py` is a working within-turn DFS on the engine's `search_begin`/`search_step` API,
and its docstring names our failure exactly: *"The generic pilot is a greedy one-action argmax: a
multi-prize lethal that needs item → attach → attack is never assembled."* It ships under
`build_submission.sh --agent rules` only. **We built the fix for greedy argmax and then shipped a
greedy argmax without it.**

Also structural: `rl/plan.py` is one-turn, single-target, committed at the first MAIN prompt and
never revised; the entire opponent model is `opp_ttk` plus two booleans. Dragapult runs a
knapsack subset enumeration over the 6-slot board (`sample-agent-dragapult/main.py:216-235`) plus
card-counting prize inference. `enumerate_plans` **cannot represent a spread-damage plan at all.**

### Constraints on any fix (ARCHITECTURE.md dead-ends)

- *"Override-style consumption of **any** eval signal on non-lethal turns — M8.1, M12, M13 — five
  measurements, all below the 0.500 null."* No sixth override-shaped consumer.
- *"the bottleneck is `score_leaf` itself, not what consumes it"* (M12). Widening solver depth on
  the current heuristic leaf is wasted.
- MCTS killed M8.4 (sims ladder flat 0.515/0.490/0.495) — but on the **v1** value head.

What differs *in kind*: make search the policy rather than a veto — `_candidate_actions` currently
beams by the greedy **rule** pilot's scorer (TOP_K=4); using the neural option head as that prior
is net-proposes/search-disposes, not override. `turn_solver.py:259` already exposes a `leaf_value`
seam and `rl/plan_iter.py` already runs `solve_turn_line` at 5× budget offline.

## 2026-07-20 ~late — WHY the win rate is ~20%: cause A confirmed, p<0.0001

Diagnostic before spending the EOD battery: are we even aiming C1 at the problem?
60 games vs `rule:dragapult`, with `rule:lucario` as a same-deck control.

### ✅ A — threat blindness. CONFIRMED.

Our entire opponent model is `opp_ttk` + two booleans: *"can their ACTIVE KO my ACTIVE"*
(`rl/plan.py:106-117`). Dragapult's Phantom Dive spreads damage to the BENCH — exactly where we
never look. Per-game share of damage on our bench:

| opponent | condition | n | median | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dragapult | **loss** | 48 | **0.318** | 0.335 |
| dragapult | win | 12 | 0.183 | 0.196 |
| lucario | loss | 39 | **0.000** | 0.117 |
| lucario | win | 18 | 0.000 | 0.097 |

Opponent-controlled (losses only, dragapult vs lucario), Mann-Whitney: **z=+5.04, p<0.0001**.

Against lucario the MEDIAN game has **zero** bench damage — our narrow threat model is adequate
there, which is why we score ~2x better (0.33 vs 0.17). Against dragapult the median loss has a
third of the damage on the bench. Within dragapult, losses (0.318) exceed wins (0.183).

Caveat: this shows dragapult spreads and we lose those games; it does not *prove* blindness causes
the loss — a perfectly-sighted agent might still lose to spread damage. But paired with the
architectural fact that our threat model cannot represent a bench threat at all, the mechanism is
concrete and actionable.

### ❌ B — prize-race denial. NOT TESTABLE as designed.

"We took a prize within 4 turns of losing": dragapult 90.4%, lucario **95.6%**. The base rate is
near-universal in ~12-turn games, so the metric cannot discriminate. My test was badly designed —
B is neither supported nor refuted. A real test needs the counterfactual (did taking that prize
hand them the race?), which is what `rl/behavior.py`-style opportunity-cost pricing is for.

### ◐ C — multi-step play. Informative, and it inverts.

Blowout share (≥5 of our prizes untaken when we lost): dragapult **38.5%**, lucario **71.1%**.
Against lucario we lose *less often but more decisively* — run over early, a setup/tempo failure.
Against dragapult we lose *more often but closer* — we are in the game and lose it late, which is
what losing to an unseen bench threat looks like. Different failure modes per opponent; a single
"fix multi-step play" story does not cover both.

### 🐛 Two bugs this diagnostic exposed

1. **`rl/behavior.py::from_series` imported `battle_finish`/`battle_select`/`battle_start` from
   `cg.api`. They live in `cg.game`.** Committed and never executed — the replay path was tested,
   the series path was not. Fixed.
2. **Seat handling.** `play_series` is slot-fair (a takes seat `g % 2`) but `game_fn` is not told
   which seat `a` received, so instrumenting `seat == 0` records the OPPONENT half the time and
   blends both win rates toward 0.5. My first run reported dragapult 0.450 / lucario 0.625 — pure
   artefact. `from_series` has the same defect and is now suspect for the same reason.
3. **Teacher instance collision.** `make_pilot(spec, instance)` requires a unique `instance` per
   live rule pilot (module-level state, `rl/teacher.py`). Reusing names across two `run()` calls in
   one process corrupted the SECOND run: lucario read 0.100 vs an authoritative 0.325.

All three produced plausible-looking numbers. Cross-checking against `rl.matchrunner play` is what
caught them.

### What this means for M22c

C1 (search) is aimed at the *multi-step* gap, which the diagnostic shows is real but
opponent-dependent. The confirmed, largest, opponent-specific defect is **A — we cannot represent
or anticipate a bench threat**, which sits in C2 (plan/opponent representation), not C1.

### Verdict: stop C1 here

Not because the effect is disproven — it is probably real and positive — but because:

1. The sweep shows **no headroom**: depth buys nothing, so there is no version of C1 that is
   worth more than ~2pp.
2. Latency is already 27ms of a 50ms budget, so there is no room to spend more either.
3. Another 6.5h of box time to resolve a ~2pp effect is poor value while **cause A is confirmed
   at p<0.0001 and completely untouched**.

C1 ships-or-not as a judgement call on a ~2pp unresolved gain that costs 15ms/move. It is
recorded, tested, and behind the `solved:` spec; nothing needs to be reverted.

**Next is C2.** The confirmed defect is representational: our opponent model asks only "can their
ACTIVE KO my ACTIVE" while dragapult spreads damage to the bench (median loss: a third of damage
on our bench; median lucario game: zero). Within-turn search cannot fix that — it would still be
scoring positions with a threat model that ignores half the board.

## 2026-07-20 EOD — C2.0 falsified as pre-registered; and a bigger finding underneath

### The kill-switch fired

`solver:lucario` vs `rule:dragapult`, n=400 × 2 seeds per arm, same binary, flag via env var:

| WHOLE_BOARD_THREAT | seeds | pooled | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| OFF | 0.225 / 0.207 | **0.2162** | 800 |
| ON | 0.207 / 0.250 | **0.2288** | 800 |

difference **+1.25pp** · MDE **5.83pp** · z=0.60 · **p=0.548**. The seed ranges **interleave**
(OFF 0.207–0.225, ON 0.207–0.250) — one ON seed exactly equals one OFF seed.

**Pre-registered decision honoured: C2 stops.** Whole-board threat awareness does not move the
solver, so we are not paying for encoder v5 + `migrate_v4_to_v5` + a PPO leg + a full re-pin on
the strength of it.

### Recorded caveat — NOT a reason to proceed

The test has a construct-validity limit I should have priced when designing it: `score_leaf`'s
counter term is `W_COUNTER = -1_000` against `W_PRIZE = 100_000`, i.e. a **tiebreak two orders of
magnitude below the prize terms**, consulted only inside the solver's ~2.3% of prompts that
override. So this tests "does whole-board threat help the solver's rare low-weight tiebreaks",
not "does a policy trained on whole-board threat features play better" — which is what C2.2 would
have built.

That is a real weakness in my test design. It is recorded here so the null is not over-read.
It is **not** grounds for building C2.2 anyway: moving the goalposts after a negative result is
the exact failure mode this milestone exists to prevent, and M21 is the cautionary case.

### 🔴 The finding underneath: it is not the neural net

`solver:` wraps `make_generic_pilot` (`turn_solver.py:541`) — **our** deck-agnostic pilot — not
the Pokémon Company's sample agent. So on the lucario deck against dragapult:

| pilot | what it is | vs rule:dragapult |
|---|---|---|
| `model:` B3 | our neural net, no search | 0.172–0.207 |
| `solved:` B3 | our neural net + within-turn search | 0.2245 |
| `solver:lucario` | our generic rule pilot + within-turn search | **0.2162** |
| `rule:lucario` | **the Pokémon Company's sample agent** | **~0.505** |

**Every pilot we have built sits at 0.17–0.22. The sample agent sits at 0.50, on the same deck.**
The deficit is not neural-specific, and it is not about within-turn search — our search pilot is
no better than our net. Something the sample agent does, none of our three pilots do.

That reframes M22c. The question is no longer "how do we improve the net" but "what does the
sample agent do that our entire pilot lineage does not" — and both are rule-based, both pilot the
same deck, and we have the source of both. That is a cheap, concrete comparison and it is the
obvious next step.
