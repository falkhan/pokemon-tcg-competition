### The meta co-gate is not a gate (11% power)

`data/kaggle/meta_v2/manifest.json` + `rl/replay_bc.py:477-486`: `meta_score` is a

|---|---|---|

| mega_kangaskhan_ex+crustle | 347 | 0.0820 |

| gate as run | n_eff/arm | MDE |
|---|---|---|
| meta_v2, 2-seed | 147 | **16.3pp** |

- **Both M21 ship decisions were noise.** B2's +4.4pp meta edge: **z = 0.68**.

  `5.83 × √2 = 8.24pp`; B2 observed 0.579 − 0.497 = **8.2pp**. That is 1.00 SD — there is no

### 🔴 KILL: neither instrument has demonstrated live-predictive validity

Predictor validity over all 7 shipped subs with cached episodes, exact permutation p-values
(unit of inference = the model, n=7; never the game — games within a model share a network):

| test | ρ | perm p | concordance |
|---|---|---|---|

The mirror→score result looked like the headline. **It is a time artifact.** Spearman vs
chronological ship order: **mirror ρ=+0.991**, live score ρ=+0.857 — we have improved mirror

> **mirror → score partial ρ = −0.037** (from +0.847). The signal vanishes entirely.
> meta → score partial ρ = −0.742.

**Verdict:** the campaign has **no validated offline→live predictor**. This is stronger and more

is that we have been selecting checkpoints on instruments never shown to measure the target.

## 2026-07-20 ~15:00 — 🔴 every gate opponent is in the training pool

Prompted by Piotr asking what "contamination" actually means. Pinning the term down surfaced a
defect nobody had noticed.

**Definition.** Contamination = train/test leakage with *opponents* as the data. If we train
against X and then gate on performance vs X, a high score no longer means "strong agent" — it can
mean "learned X's quirks." The gate stops being a test and becomes a training-progress readout.

**Audit of B3's live mixture (`runs/m21_legB3.log:78`) against our gates:**

| gate | opponent | weight in B3 training |
|---|---|---|
| mirror (canonical) | `solver:lucario` | **0.45** |
| meta co-gate | the 4 meta decks | 0.55 |
| floor | `random:kyogre` | 0.05 |
| floor | `rule:lucario` | 0.05 |

**Nothing is held out. Not one gate.**

### 🐛 The 0.45 is a double-count, and it is a real bug

The mixture lists `solver:data/kaggle/meta_v2/deck_20dcd3130bc0.csv=0.30` and
`solver:lucario=0.15` as if they were two different opponents. They are **the same opponent** —
`deck_20dcd3130bc0.csv` is byte-identical to `decks/lucario.csv` (md5 `aef8da62…`), same solver
pilot. So **45% of B3's training was against our solver piloting our exact deck**, written as two
entries, and the mirror gate measures precisely that.

The same collapse that made the meta gate identical to the mirror gate had *already happened
inside the training mixture*, unnoticed. The declared weights do not mean what they say.

**Action for M22b:** rewrite the mixture so weights are deduplicated by resolved (deck, pilot)
identity, and consciously reallocate the freed mass. `parse_pool` (`rl/collector.py:119-148`)
should reject or merge duplicate resolved specs rather than silently summing them.

### Two problems were hiding under one word

1. **Leakage** — trained on the evaluator. *Fixable by holding something out.*
2. **Endogeneity** — the evaluator is our own artifact (our solver, our past selves). Beating it
   says nothing about other teams' agents, however carefully it is held out. *Not fixable by
   holding out.*

`rule:lucario`, `random:kyogre`, `solver:iono`, `generic` and the meta decks are contaminated in
sense 1. Everything solver-piloted is endogenous in sense 2 regardless.

**`dragapult` is the only opponent we own that is clean on both** — never in `collector.py`,
`plan_iter.py` or `ppo.py`, and written by The Pokémon Company rather than by us.

**Correction to the previous recommendation:** holding out `drakloak` as a replacement evaluator
was proposed as "strictly better, since it appears in the live field." That was wrong — a
held-out drakloak is still *solver-piloted*, so it fixes leakage only and stays inside the closed
loop. It is a partial substitute, not an equivalent one. There is no way to manufacture another
doubly-clean opponent: we cannot write an agent we did not write.

### Decision (Piotr, 07-20): dragapult stays OUT of training

Reversing the earlier "add it with sequencing" plan. Dragapult is reserved as the out-of-loop
evaluator for at least one more leg, and used to test whether M22b's PPO scaling generalizes
beyond the closed loop. Training against it would spend a non-renewable instrument to buy
opponent diversity we can approximate with held-out harvested archetypes.

### ⚠️ The caveat that keeps this honest, and is baked into the tool's output

Flags are counted over **whole games**, so they are confounded with game state: a game you are
winning has more turns, more energy and a wider board, which moves flag rates for reasons that
have nothing to do with causation. **A negative delta does not mean the behaviour helps.**

So this is a *filter, not a cause-finder*. It can refute "flag X explains our losses"; it cannot
establish that X helps or hurts. `report_flags` prints that caveat unconditionally and a test
pins it (`test_report_always_carries_the_confounding_caveat`), so the table cannot be quoted
without it.
