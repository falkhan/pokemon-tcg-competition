# M2 — Beyond imitation: the BC ceiling and the pivot to MCTS

**Date:** 2026-07-08 (in progress)
**Status:** 🚧 Active. PPO scaffold + core built and validated; deck-quality measured;
BC-rebase attempted and rejected on evidence; **pivoted to AlphaGo-Zero-style MCTS**.
**Plan:** [M2-plan.md](M2-plan.md)

M2's goal is to get *beyond* imitation — a stronger policy AND a strong deck it knows how
to pilot. The milestone became a sequence of measurements that each redirected the plan.
This diary records the reasoning, because the dead ends are the most useful part.

## Phase 0 — eval fairness + throughput (done)

- **Slot swapping** (`rl/eval.play_games`): the engine gives player-0 a large edge —
  teacher-mirror measured **61%** without swapping, **47%** with. Every head-to-head
  number in M2 is slot-fair; the M1 "70% vs teacher" was inflated by this bias.
- **Parallel collector** (`rl/collector.py`): multiprocessing, direct engine loop,
  **~53,000 games/hr** (10× the plan's 5k target). This throughput is what made the
  measurement-driven approach below cheap enough to run.

## The reorder (Piotr's objection)

A 5-iteration PPO smoke test on the fixed Kyogre deck confirmed the code learns (ratio≈1,
entropy healthy) but barely moved (vs_best 50%). Piotr pushed back: a long single-deck
*mirror* run is premature because (a) mirror self-play is ~50/50 by construction, so most
of the advantage signal is coin-flip noise (value_loss floored at ~0.35 — you can't
predict a fair coin); (b) the agent sees only ONE deck and only copies of itself, but the
leaderboard is full of other decks; (c) our warm start pilots an *unverified* deck. So we
stopped and measured instead of training.

## Deck quality — the round-robin (Phase 0.5)

Three archetype packages (deck + its competent rule pilot), slot-fair, n=100. Built
`decks/{kyogre,lucario,iono}.csv`, extracted the Iono rule agent to `sample-agent-iono/`,
and generalized `rl/teacher.load_teacher(name, agent=, deck=)`.

| Matchup | Result |

| Kyogre (bc_v1) vs Iono (rule) | **12%** |
| Lucario (rule) vs Iono (rule) | **76%** |

Ranking: **Lucario ≫ Iono ≫ our current agent.** Our shipped M1 agent is the *weakest* of

| Isolation test | Result | Reading |

| bc_v1 vs rule-generic, both on Kyogre | 51% | **pilot is fine** — bc_v1 ties its generic teacher |
| Kyogre vs Lucario, both under the rule brain | 30% | **the deck is the problem** |

## The BC rebase — attempted and REJECTED on evidence

Conclusion seemed obvious: rebase on Lucario (strongest deck, and it has an *expert* rule
demonstrator, unlike Kyogre which only ever got generic fallback play). Re-ran the M1 BC
pipeline on Lucario-expert + Lucario-deck → `bc_lucario`. It was **worse**:

| bc_lucario vs | Win rate |
|---|---|
| CURRENT (bc_v1+Kyogre) | **38%** (loses to our own current agent) |
| Lucario expert | **29%** (captured ~half the expert it cloned) |
| Iono expert | 13% |

Imitation accuracy told the story in advance: **55%** on Lucario vs **90%** on Kyogre.

## THE key lesson of M2 so far

**Behavior cloning cannot imitate reasoning it cannot observe.** The Lucario expert's
strength lives in *hidden-state lookahead* — its `AttackPlan` simulates attacks and picks
targets across turns (see `sample-agent/main.py`). That plan is not in the observation, so
to a state-only imitator the expert's best moves look partly arbitrary. Result:

> **agent strength ≈ teacher strength × imitation fidelity**, and fidelity *collapses*
> when the teacher reasons with unobservable state.

So a **high-fidelity clone of a weak-but-simple pilot (Kyogre, 90%) beats a low-fidelity
clone of a strong-but-complex pilot (Lucario, 55%)** — exactly backwards from intuition.
Corollary: BC has a hard ceiling here. All our clones sit *below* the rule experts
(bc_v1+Kyogre loses to them 22%/12%); you cannot imitate your way past them.

Do NOT ship bc_lucario. `rl/export.py` default reverted to the champion bc_v1+Kyogre
(and rewritten to always ship a matched (checkpoint, deck) pair — the M1 "one artifact"
lesson, now enforced).

## The pivot — AlphaGo-Zero-style MCTS

Breaking the BC ceiling needs either *learning* the hidden play through outcomes (PPO) or
*doing* the lookahead at inference (search). Piotr chose **search**, correctly: MCTS
supplies at decision time exactly the lookahead BC can't imitate.

Architecture note (prompted by the AlphaGo Zero paper): original AlphaGo used two separate
networks (policy + value); **AlphaGo Zero unified them into one two-headed net**, with MCTS
as a "policy improvement operator" (raw policy → search → sharper policy, distilled back).
**Our `OptionScorer` is already that two-headed design**, and its value head was
pre-trained on ±1 outcomes during BC precisely for leaf evaluation. Two deliberate
differences from Zero: we **warm-start from BC** (not tabula rasa — a pragmatic head start,
like original AlphaGo's SL bootstrap), and we need **determinized MCTS** because the game
is imperfect-information (guess the opponent's hidden hand/deck/prizes, search the concrete
game, average over the noise — the official sample fills the opponent with Snorlax + basic
energy). Bonus: MCTS visit-count targets are a far stronger *training* signal than PPO's
noisy advantages, which may also fix the mirror-self-play SNR problem above.

## Forward-model feasibility — validated

Before building MCTS, verified the engine's forward model (`cg.api.search_begin` /
`search_step`) works with our determinized guesses:
- `search_begin` accepts sampled hidden state, returns a searchable root with options;
  `search_step` rolls plies forward to terminal. ✔ (training env)
- The **kaggle env passes `search_begin_input` to the active agent** (`cabt.py:187`), so
  MCTS can run *inside the submission* at inference, not only in training. ✔ (ship path)
