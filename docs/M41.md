### Three card-data traps the lab caught on first contact

1. **`is_ex` and `is_mega_ex` are disjoint** (121 / 30, zero overlap) — "an ex"
   is `is_ex or is_mega_ex`.
2. **Evolution is by NAME, not id.** `decks/lucario.csv` runs Riolu #677 while
   Mega Lucario ex points at #974, so an id-membership check reports a false
   "missing basic" on a deck we ship.
3. **Cinderace starts from hand.** All 237 harvested `archaludon` seats run 4x
   Cinderace (Stage 2, from Raboot) with no Raboot and no Rare Candy — because
   its "Explosiveness" reads *"if this Pokémon is in your hand when you are
   setting up to play, you may put it face down in the Active Spot."* Without
   that exemption the lab errors on the most-played build of a whole archetype.
   Detected from ability text, with the id as a fallback so behaviour is
   identical under `tests/fake_cg.py`.

### The real finding: `_best_damage` cannot see our own attacker

Building the fixed-pilot deck probe, `generic:alakazam_v2_h4` won **1 game in
60** against everything — while `generic:lucario` went 0.425 vs the same grim
deck. Cause: `rl/combat.py::_best_damage` reads an attack's **printed** damage.

Our deck's entire plan is **Alakazam #743 `Powerful Hand`, printed damage 0**,
which really places 2 damage counters per card in hand — **20 x hand size**, so
340 with the 17-card hands this deck actually holds. The rule pilot therefore
ranks our own win condition below **Dudunsparce at 90** and plays our deck as a
Dudunsparce deck.

**This reaches the shipped agent.** `rl/encoders.py::_combat_features` builds 11
features with the same `_best_damage` and feeds them to the net; `rl/plan.py:118`
uses it for return-KO estimation. Measured directly: with Alakazam active it
hands the net

    my_dmg = 0        my_ko = 0.0        my_can_attack = 0.0

Pool-wide, **174 of 1556 attacks (11.2%) scale, and 142 of those print ≤30** —
the model treats them as near-harmless. Partially known and never followed up:
`docs/M36-plan.md:450` has a one-line note ("`_attack_damage` blind to variable
attacks") from the gust-veto verification, never quantified and never connected
to the encoder or to the fact that it is our own deck's plan.

### But the policy compensated — measured, not assumed

`scripts/m41_scaling_probe.py` over 158 cached live episodes of subs
55172160 / 55182097 / 55185485:

| | |
|---|---|
| turns with Alakazam active and ATTACK legal | **617** |
| of those, we attacked | **617 (100.0%)** |
| of those, `_best_damage` said "can attack" | **0 (0.0%)** |
| turns where 20 × hand ≥ their remaining HP | 534 |
| lethal turns we attacked on | **534 (100.0%) — 0 missed** |
| control: other attackers active, ATTACK legal | 148 turns, attacked 77.7% |

The feature says "this Pokémon cannot attack" on 100% of those turns and the
policy attacks on 100% of them — and more reliably than with flat-damage
attackers (100% vs 77.7%). **BC from real winners plus the raw hand-size and
HP inputs is enough to learn the scaling the engineered feature hides.** So
this is a wrong-and-wasted input feature, not a live defect: no ship, no gate,
no urgency. Logged to BACKLOG for a future encoder pass.

**Instrument correction, caught before it was reported as a result.** The first
cut of the probe counted PROMPTS and reported *1,046 missed lethals*. A turn
holds several prompts but allows only one attack, so every play-a-card prompt
in a turn that ended in an attack scored as a declined attack. Per TURN the
number is **0 missed of 534**. Same failure shape as the four M40 instrument
errors: a new instrument gave a confident wrong answer before the code under
test did.

### UI pass (Piotr's review of the first build)

The first build was correct and unusable: an empty deck rendered six red error
boxes before you had added a card, the table was undifferentiated text, and
card selection needed a second dropdown under it. Four changes:

- **Legality is asserted only on Save.** While building, the sidebar shows a
  progress bar (`n / 60`), a kind breakdown and the deck's energy identity —
  progress, not judgement. `Save` validates name + legality, writes nothing if
  either fails, and reports each reason as `st.error`. Advisory notes
  (evolution lines, energy coverage) moved to a collapsed expander in the Deck
  tab. An empty deck now produces zero error boxes; pressing Save on it
  produces exactly the two real ones.
- **✨ New deck** starts an empty deck in memory; nothing reaches disk until Save.
- **Click a row to inspect it.** `st.dataframe(on_select="rerun",
  selection_mode="single-row")` replaces the dropdown; the detail pane renders
  under a colour bar in the card's own energy hue.
- **Icons and colour.** Energy symbols per type (🌿🔥💧⚡🔮👊🌑⚙️🐉⭐), trainer
  glyphs (🎒🔧🧑🏟️🔋), tier badges (🌟 Mega ex, ✨ ex, 💎 Tera, 🅰 ACE SPEC), HP
  and damage as proportional bars. Colours are `ENERGY_COLORS` lifted verbatim
  from `notebooks/card_pool_eda.ipynb`, where they were validated
  CVD-separable — and because that palette carries two residual WARNs
  (Fire↔Lightning ΔE 7.9, Colorless contrast 2.45:1), the type name and symbol
  always appear alongside the colour. A test pins that every energy type has
  both a colour and a symbol, and that no card in the pool renders blank.

### Save-destination clarity (Piotr: "so it's clearer that saving doesn't overwrite an existing deck")

A **✨ New deck / 📂 From existing** segmented control now owns the sidebar's top
slot. New hides the source picker and starts blank; From existing shows it and
captions `editing a copy of decks/<x>.csv`. Under the name field the exact
destination is always printed — `saves to decks/custom/<name>.csv` — and turns
into a ⚠️ when that file already exists.

Three real protections behind the cosmetics:

- `save_deck` raises `FileExistsError` unless `overwrite=True`; the app turns
  that into an error plus an explicit `⚠️ Overwrite <name>.csv` button. Saving
  twice cannot silently discard the first version.
- `custom_deck_path` is the only path builder and is `NAME_RE`-gated, so
  `../lucario`, `a/b` and `..\lucario` cannot escape `decks/custom/`. Curated
  decks in `decks/` and harvested lists in `data/kaggle/` are structurally
  unreachable, not merely avoided by convention.
- `suggest_name` returns the first free `stem`, `stem_2`, … so loading
  `decks/lucario.csv` proposes `lucario`, and once that copy exists, `lucario_2`.

**Bug this surfaced, found by driving the app rather than reading it.** After
Load, the destination caption still said `untitled.csv`: a keyed widget owns its
value after first render, so the `value=` argument is ignored from then on and
`w_deck_name` kept the stale name while `deck_name` held the new one. The fix is
to make the widget key the single source of truth and assign it from the Load
callback (legal — callbacks run before widgets re-render). Verified end to end:
Load lucario → name `lucario`, destination `decks/custom/lucario.csv`, save
lands there, and `decks/lucario.csv` is untouched in git.

134 lab tests, 920 suite-wide (same 4 known failures).

## 2026-08-03 (later) — scaling damage fixed; the probe runs, and Grass beats grim

`rl/scaling.py` (NEW) is a curated `attackId -> (mode, per_unit, base)` table in
the spirit of `combat.SPREAD_ATTACKS` / `CONDITIONAL_ATTACKS`: rules text cannot
be parsed reliably, so the handful of scaling attacks the census says decide
real matchups are encoded by hand. Wired in as an **opt-in fix token**, the
pattern the repo already uses for pilot variants — `generic-scale:` /
`solver-scale:` spec kinds in `_FIXED_KINDS`, off by default.

**The shipped agent is untouched by construction**: `score_attack(scaling=False)`
is the default, nothing in `submission/` imports `rl.scaling`, and the four
bundled twins are byte-identical. That matters because the live net was
BC-trained against the current (wrong) features and demonstrably learned around
them — changing what it is fed without retraining would be a silent
distribution shift for no measured gain.

**A first draft of the table guessed two of its three ids** and pointed at
Trapinch and Magcargo ex instead of the cards its own comments named. Caught by
printing the engine's rules text for each id before wiring it in.
`tests/test_scaling.py` now re-derives every entry from the card PARQUETS
(engine-free, so it runs under the conftest stub) and asserts each attack's text
actually contains "for each".

Curated entries, all verified against `cg.api`:

### Second instrument bug, same session: the weighting was not comparable

The probe excluded mirror cells "because they are 0.5 by symmetry". That drops a
DIFFERENT slice of the field per candidate — grim's own mirror is **49.5%** of
the top 250 while ogerpon's is 3.6%, so it was comparing
grim-minus-its-hardest-matchup against everyone else's full slate. It read
grim 0.814 vs grass 0.816, i.e. a tie. Including mirrors (they measure ~0.50
anyway) puts every candidate on the same 90.1% of the field:

| candidate | weighted vs top-250 field | vs grim alone |
|---|---|---|
| **grass (Teal Mask Ogerpon ex)** | **0.776 ±0.035** | **0.750 ±0.060** |
| grim (Marnie's Grimmsnarl) | 0.659 ±0.039 | — |
| wall (Great Tusk / Crustle) | 0.376 ±0.029 | 0.138 ±0.048 |
| ours (Alakazam) | 0.162 ±0.021 | 0.040 ±0.027 |

So the biased weighting hid an 11.7pp gap. **Grass beats grim head-to-head
0.750 under an identical pilot** — the printed-weakness prediction (the whole
Grimmsnarl line is weak to Grass) survives its first measurement.

**Do not read `ours 0.162` as evidence about our deck.** It is the documented
rule-pilot artifact: even with the scaling fix the pilot gets Alakazam to the
Active spot ~1.7 times a game against ~3.9 live, because the deck's plan needs
setup play a weak generalist does not execute. The probe measures "how well does
a simple pilot do with these 60 cards" — a lower bound, and a fair comparison
only between decks a simple pilot can actually operate.

**Not actionable yet.** n=200/cell resolves ~7pp and the run is single-seed;
G-12 says `--workers 8` is not run-reproducible. Needs n>=800 with replication
before it informs a deck decision.

### Fill with energy

A `🔋 Fill N slots with <type>` button under the sidebar progress bar tops a
deck up to 60 with basic energy of the type it needs. The type is inferred from
**attack demand** (copies-weighted energy symbols the deck must actually pay),
falling back to the Pokemon's own types for an all-Colorless deck. Only the
eight printed basic energies exist — there is no basic Colorless or Dragon — so
those types are skipped rather than silently mis-filled, and basic energy's
exemption from the 4-copy rule means any shortfall can be covered (kyogre needs
35).

Validation that the inference is real rather than plausible: strip the energy
out of six shipped decks and the attack costs alone lead back to the type each
author chose — lucario→Fighting, grim→Darkness, alakazam→Psychic,
iono→Lightning, archaludon→Metal, kyogre→Water. Six for six, pinned as tests.

**A pre-existing state bug this surfaced.** Adding a card from Browse that was
already in the deck left the Deck tab's `cnt_<id>` number_input showing the old
count — `＋1` on a 13-copy energy gave `deck=14, widget=13`, and the stale
widget would have pushed 13 back on the next interaction. Same root cause as
the deck-name bug: a keyed widget owns its value after first render. Fixed with
`_set_count`, which writes the deck and the widget key together, plus
`_forget_counts` on load/new/clear so a previous deck's widgets cannot leak into
the next one. The count widget no longer takes a `value=` at all — the key is
the value, seeded once.

## 2026-08-03 (later still) — the replay bridge closes the review loop

`scripts/watch_games.py` (NEW). `rl.matchrunner` drives `cg.game` directly for
speed and so never builds the `visualize` payload the official viewer needs —
you get a win rate and nothing to look at. `rl.eval.play_games` goes through
`kaggle_environments`, which does build it, but was written for shipped file
agents. They join cleanly and nobody had joined them: `play_games` accepts
CALLABLES, and `make_pilot` returns `(agent(obs_dict), deck_ids)` — exactly the
kaggle agent contract, including returning the deck when `obs.select is None`.

So any spec matchrunner understands is now watchable, and one run gives both
halves of a review loop:

    replays/<tag>_NNN.html        a self-contained page per game whose button
                                  POSTs to ptcgvis — "watch it and spot the
                                  obvious mistake"
    replays/<tag>/ep_gNNN_aS.json cached-episode shape, read directly by
                                  `python -m rl.postmortem --batch` — "is it
                                  systematic"

    uv run python scripts/watch_games.py --a generic-scale:decks/alakazam_v2_h4.csv \
        --b generic-scale:data/kaggle/grim_3121746f_deck.csv -n 4 --postmortem

Verified end to end: 4 games, 4 valid pages (2.7 MB each, `const vis = [...]`
embedded, ptcgvis form action present), manifest and index regenerated, and the

**And its first run corroborated the probe's documented limitation without

## 2026-08-03 — PILOT vs DECK, decomposed: the heuristics pivot is refuted by measurement

Piotr raised moving away from cloning and investing in the rule pilot +
heuristics instead, on the grounds that we may lack the time and compute to
teach a net from scratch. Three n=400 cells decompose it. Same engine, same
seeds, slot-swapped.

| cell | matchup | wr | ELO |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | net(ours) vs **rule**(ours) — deck held constant | 0.945 ±0.022 | **+494** |
| C | rule(ogerpon) vs rule(ours) — **pilot** held constant | 0.900 ±0.029 | **+382** |
| B | rule(ogerpon) vs **net**(ours) — best deck + dumb pilot vs our stack | 0.198 ±0.039 | **−243** |

**The pilot gap is +112 ELO LARGER than the deck gap**, and cell B is the
decision: our net on a mid-tier deck beats the deck-agnostic rule pilot on the
**#1 deck on the ladder**, 0.802. Replacing the net with heuristics would
surrender ~+494 to gain at most ~+382 — and B says the realised gain is far
less than that.

So the pivot as stated is refuted. But it is right in a different form: the
campaign's best measured ROI has been the RULES LAYER **on top of** the net —
M39's `conserve`, one token in a config string, paid **+115 live ELO**
(664.8 → 780.4). "More heuristics" is a layer over the policy, not a
replacement for it.

### And a caveat this puts on the whole deck probe

Cell C measures ogerpon's deck edge at +382 ELO **weak pilot on both sides**.
Cell B runs the same ogerpon list against a competent opponent and it manages
only 0.198. Deck advantage does not transfer intact against a strong pilot: a
good deck is worth much more when your opponent is also bad at defending it.

Every number `scripts/deck_probe.py` produces is measured in that weak-vs-weak
world, so its deck effects are an UPPER bound. That is now two independent
reasons to treat the probe as a lower-bound screen rather than a deck oracle —
this one, and the piloting-complexity confound (its ranking tracks distinct
Pokemon count and Stage-2 lines, and it scores an archetype sitting at ladder
rank #6 at 0.175).

### The Fire idea, checked and corrected

All six "Fire" teams in the top 250 are **Dragapult** decks — the census's own
replay-evidence column reads `played=Dragapult ex` for every one, including the
rank-#11 list our decklist heuristic labelled "Blaziken ex". Blaziken ex is a
2-of TECH inside a Dragapult shell, never a main attacker. So "Fire has the
highest mean score (1056)" is evidence for Dragapult, not for a Fire deck.
Worth keeping: **Dragapult ex has no weakness at all**, which is the
general-purpose property the shell is built on.
