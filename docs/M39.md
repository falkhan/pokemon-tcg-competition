## 2026-08-01 — P0.7 strip ledger, AND A CORRECTION to the P0.8 entry above

### The methodological finding that forced a re-measurement

The first ablation pass (n=800/cell) produced `plain` = **0.466** on the top
bed. The P0.8 gap battery, **same specs, same bed, same seeds**, had
produced **0.516**. Two independent n=800 measurements of one quantity, 5pp
apart.

Cause, confirmed by running the identical command five times at seed 1:
**0.471 / 0.494 / 0.526 / 0.506 / 0.537**. `matchrunner --workers 8` is not
run-reproducible — M37 established this (engine RNG is per worker process;
`mp.Pool` assigns chunks by timing) and it is written in
`scripts/m37_decide.py` B4. The spread is ordinary binomial, not excess
variance, but the consequence is sharp: **the seed does not pin the result,
so every cell is an independent sample and n=800 buys ±3.5pp.**

Cross-referenced against [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)'s own power table,
detecting 5pp needs **n≈1560 per arm**. The ablation was therefore
underpowered for the effects it was built to find — its "no signal"
verdicts were preordained, not informative. Re-ran the decision-relevant
arms to **n=2400/cell**.

## 2026-08-01 — D1 RESULT: the problem was never saturation. **Bed strength is a training lottery.**

D1 built `m39_bc_grim_vsus` — the grim list cloned from ONLY the seats where
grim faced alakazam, using the new `--opp-deck-hash` filter
(`rl/replay_bc.py`, +2 tests). First surprise: the filter dropped just **7 of
195 seats**. The unfiltered grim bed was **already ~96% matchup-specific**,
so "the clone never learned our matchup" is dead — it learned almost nothing
else.

Second surprise, and the real result. Three clones of the **same grim list**,
from **essentially the same data**, played against the same net (cont3) at
n=2400 each:

| clone | corpus | epochs | our WR vs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `m39_bc_grim` | 195 seats | 10 | **0.6713** ±0.019 |
| `m39_bc_grim_b` | 195 seats (identical) | **9** | **0.6233** ±0.019 |
| `m39_bc_grim_vsus` | 188 seats (96% overlap) | 10 | **0.5460** ±0.020 |

**Spread 12.5pp against a binomial CI of ±2.0pp.** One fewer training epoch,
same data, moves bed strength 4.8pp. A 4% corpus change moves it 12.5pp.

**Bed play-strength is not a stable property of a corpus — it is a draw from
a lottery over training runs**, with a spread ~6× the sampling CI we have
been quoting.

### This dissolves the last two entries

- **"Saturation"** (already refuted by the head-to-head) is fully explained:
  `m39_bc_grim` 0.708 vs `m39_bc_topgrim` 0.699 looked like "band doesn't
  transfer", but those were two single draws from a ±6pp lottery landing
  near each other by chance. The head-to-head (topgrim beats grim 0.623,
  z=+12.04) was the true signal.
- **The bed-vs-live grim gap** needs no exotic explanation either. Our bed
  was a weak draw; a different draw of the same list gives 0.546, much
  closer to what the (7-game, CI [0.03,0.51]) live cell suggested.

### What is and is not damaged

**Safe — ship decisions.** The gate compares arm vs control on the SAME
fixed bed, so the bed's lottery draw is a constant that cancels in the
delta. **Ship A's +1.74pp (z=+3.42) stands.** Residual risk is second-order:
a rule could in principle help against a weak grim draw and not a strong
one, which the fix below also addresses.

**Damaged — every absolute bed number.** "We beat grim at 0.70", and by
extension P0.8's "we are only 2.7pp from the 900+ band", carry ~±6pp of
unquantified bed-construction variance on top of the ±2pp sampling CI. The
ceiling reading should be treated as one draw, not a measurement.

### The fix, and it is free

**Beds must be PANELS, not single nets.** Train K clones per family
(different epochs/splits — training is ~2 min) and gate against all K,
reporting the panel mean. Bed variance then falls as sqrt(K).

The cost is zero, because it is a *redistribution* of match budget rather
than an addition: instead of n=2400 against one draw, run n=800 against each
of three draws. Same compute, same total n, but the number now averages over
the lottery instead of sampling it once. Every bed battery in this milestone
spent its budget the wrong way.

Proposed **G-13: no bed is a single clone.** Gate rosters use >=3 draws per
family, the panel mean is the cell value, and the spread across draws is
reported — a family whose draws disagree by more than ~10pp is telling you
its cell is not measuring what you think.

## 2026-08-02 — P3 corpus B: THE PLAN'S RECIPE WAS WRONG, and it would have re-run a known kill

The plan specifies corpus B as "`plan_iter collect` against the beds, keep
the winning seats, low-dose fine-tune — **no new infrastructure**". Reading
the code before running it:

`plan_iter collect` labels every row with `_teacher_step` — the SOLVER
teacher — in **both** `expert` and `ei` mode
([plan_iter.py:388](../rl/plan_iter.py:388)). In `ei` mode the student only
*executes*; it never *labels*. A corpus collected that way is a
solver-teacher corpus, and **M38's central negative result is that
solver-teacher labels cannot beat the winners lineage at any dose**
(.123–.264 against a .517–.631 control). Running the plan as written would
have spent the milestone's headline lane re-deriving a kill we already own,
under a new name.

What best-response needs is the opposite label source: **our own net's
executed actions in the games it won**. `scripts/m39_collect_bestresp.py`
(NEW) does that — it wraps the matchrunner pilot, records every own-seat
decision with the *same encoder calls* `replay_bc.encode_decisions` uses (so
corpus B is schema- and encoding-identical to corpus A and to the champion
shards), and writes `results` per row so `--outcome-weight 0` performs the
winners filter at train time.

**One deliberate addition beyond the plan, with its reason.** Greedy
self-imitation is nearly a no-op: the labels would be the net's own argmax,
so the cross-entropy gradient at those rows is already near zero and the
"fine-tune" would do little more than re-weight states. The collector
therefore explores — with probability `--eps` (0.08) it substitutes a
uniformly random legal option on a single-pick MAIN prompt and records THAT.
Exploration plus an outcome filter is the mechanism by which imitation can
exceed the policy it imitates; without it corpus B does not test the plan's
hypothesis at all.

**Limitation, stated up front:** this is *filtered self-imitation*, the
offline approximation of best-response, not best-response (that needs a
policy gradient against the bed — BACKLOG sweep #7, ~327M env steps). Its
ceiling is our own net's best explored play, which is exactly why corpus A
(demonstrators to 1058) runs in parallel. Which one wins says which
constraint was binding — the pre-registered A/B diagnostic.

Collection runs against **six beds — the three loss families × two panel
draws each** (G-13 applied to corpus construction too: a corpus manufactured
against a single weak draw would teach lines that only beat that draw).
